# Play

> Summary: Shows a level on the board and lets the player drag blocks out through their doors. The play session shows the level (board pieces from pools, the camera framed to the board's size) and runs the level's time; the board interaction takes the board's touches from the EventSystem, turns them into board positions, asks the level attempt for whole-cell steps and a sub-cell offset, and moves the block views. When the level is won or failed, and the last block has finished sliding out, the session tells its caller once. Over the board the HUD shows the level number, the time left and the coins, with restart and pause; the Game scene opens the level complete popup (the coin reward, then the next level) or the level failed popup (retry or home), and pause opens the settings popup over the stopped level.

## Code
- Folder: `Assets/Game/Runtime/Play/` (`Scripts/Flow/`, `Scripts/Presentation/` with `Board/`, `Blocks/`, `Camera/`, `Hud/`, `Input/`, `Scripts/UI/`, `Scripts/Settings/`; assets in `Installers/`, `Settings/`, `Prefabs/` with `Board/`, `Blocks/`, `UI/`, `Materials/` with `Board/`, `Blocks/`, `UI/`; `Shaders/`), `Assets/Game/Runtime/Bootstrap/` (`GameScope`, `GameEntry`, `Scenes/Game.unity`), the outline's fork in `Assets/Plugins/QuickOutline/` (assembly `QuickOutline`)
- Assembly: `Game`
- Entry points: `PlaySession.Start(level, onWon, onFailed)`, `Pause()`, `Resume()`, `RemainingSeconds`, `IsDecided`, `Use(booster, overlay, shots)`, `IsAvailable(booster)` (`Play/Scripts/Flow/PlaySession.cs`; the boosters in [Boosters](Boosters.md)); the screens `LevelHud`, `LevelCompletePopup`, `LevelFailedPopup` (`Play/Scripts/UI/`), listed in `Play/Settings/PlayScreens.asset`; `PlayInstaller` (`Play/Installers/PlayInstaller.asset`). `BoardInteraction` (`Play/Scripts/Flow/BoardInteraction.cs`) is used only by the session. `LevelStage.Show` (`Play/Scripts/Presentation/Board/LevelStage.cs`) is also called by the level editor's scene preview ([Level editor](LevelEditor.md)), so the preview shows a level the way the game does.

## How it works
- **Scene.** `Game.unity` holds the camera (with a `PhysicsRaycaster`), a light, the game's `GameUIRoot` ([App flow](AppFlow.md)), the `Board` object with `BoardRoot` (the level is built under it; its idle object holds the pooled pieces no level uses) and the board's input (a `BoxCollider` and `BoardInputView` on the same object), and `GameScope`, a child of the Boot scope ([App flow](AppFlow.md)), which registers Core UI on the scene's `UIRoot`, the camera, the board root and the board input and runs its installer list (`PlayInstaller`, `PreferencesInstaller`). `GameEntry` is the scene's flow, one method per step, the screens' buttons leading from one to the next:
  - **Start** chooses the run once ([App flow](AppFlow.md)): the level the level editor asked to play (`EditorPlayRequest`, taken once; never in a build) or the player's own run. It opens the HUD and plays the run's level.
  - **Play** starts the session with the run's level and shows the HUD with the level number, the coin balance, restart (Play again) and pause. Restart and pause do nothing once the session `IsDecided` (won or failed, the last block perhaps still sliding out), so neither can throw away a win before its popup opens. Restart pauses the session, so the time cannot run out under the fade, and plays the level again covered.
  - **OfferBooster** is the HUD's call for a booster pressed at zero stock: it pauses the session and opens the buy popup; buying or closing resumes ([Boosters](Boosters.md)). A booster pressed with stock goes from the HUD straight to the session and is no step of the flow ([Decisions](../Decisions.md)).
  - **Pause** pauses the session and opens the settings popup ([Preferences](Preferences.md)); its close resumes the level, its home goes to the Home scene.
  - **Won** opens the level complete popup with the reward of `WinRewardSettings`. Its continue adds the coins to the wallet and advances the run at once, both saved before anything moves; then `GameEntry` asks the Coins feature's `CoinFlight` to show the paid coins flying from the popup's continue button to the HUD's counter.
  - **Failed** opens the level failed popup; retry plays the same level again covered, home goes to the Home scene.
  - **Covered** (`PlayCoveredAsync`): the screen fades to black through Core's `ISceneTransition`, the same fade as a scene switch ([Decisions](../Decisions.md)), the popup that led here is closed under the black, the level is played and the screen fades back in. Retry, restart and continue all take this path. The only awaited work in the flow; a token cancelled when the scope is disposed ends it with the scene.
  All of it happens in this scene, without a scene switch, until home is chosen.
- **Layout.** `BoardLayout` puts the board on the XZ plane centered on the origin, row 0 at the far edge (+Z). It works in the board root's local space, the space the board is built in: it converts cells and drag poses to local positions and local points to board positions in cells. It is made from the art settings, so it also holds the board's box (`Box`: the board with its frame, from the ground to its highest part, the blocks' tops or the door arrows' top on the frame, whichever is higher), the one box the camera frames and the input covers.
- **Showing a level.** `LevelStage.Show(level, parent, pieces, camera)` is the one way a level is shown: it makes the layout, builds the board under the parent from the piece source, tells the block shader where the board is (`BoardClip`: the board root's world-to-local matrix and the half size of the board with its frame as shader globals) and frames the camera. It makes its `BoardBuilder` (with its `BlockPieceBuilder`) and `CameraFraming` from the settings assets it is given, so the level editor names only `LevelStage`, the settings and a piece source, and a new dependency of the builder or the framing changes one constructor. The caller owns the parent and the pieces: the session builds under the scene's board root from the pools, the scene preview under its own unsaved object from plain copies.
- **Building.** `BoardBuilder.Build(level, layout, parent, pieces)` places a ground tile per cell, the frame (one `DoorView` per door with an arrow on its top, one wall piece per run of cells between doors and corners, a corner piece on each corner) and one `BlockView` per block under the parent, in its local space, and returns the layout with the block views and the door views, each in level order (`StagedLevel`). It takes every piece, block and door from the `IBoardPieceSource` it is given (`Take(prefab, parent)`, an instance active under the parent with the prefab's local pose; a pooled source reuses instances, so whoever gave the builder the source releases what was taken, its own way) and creates, destroys and remembers nothing itself. `BlockPieceBuilder` builds a block from the supplied quarter pieces ([Supplied assets](../SuppliedAssets.md)): the piece of each quarter follows from its side and diagonal neighbours, and one inner-corner piece covers the three quarters around a concave corner; a quarter piece is placed at its quarter's center and an inner corner at its concave corner, turned about that point. Frame pieces are turned to face out of their side. The supplied wall and door pieces are straight extrusions along their length (their vertices lie only on their two ends), so each is stretched along its cells and the frame reads as smooth bars, with no seam between the cells of a door or a wall run; the door arrow stands on the door's top at its middle, turned with the door so it points out of the board. Each corner gets the rounded corner piece, turned so its rounded corner points away from the board. Under the ground tiles lies one ground base, a ground tile stretched over the board and half the wall around it and set a little lower, so it shows as thin lines between the tiles. The board's proportions are tuning in `BoardArtSettings`, in model units: the block lift raises or sinks the blocks (0 with the flat plates of `BlockPlates.fbx`), the frame lift raises walls and doors (the pieces are deep enough to stay below the ground), and the wall and door thicknesses scale a frame piece along its outward axis, with its inner face kept on the board's edge, so a thicker door stands out of the wall on the outside only; a corner piece takes the wall's thickness both ways.
- **Pieces.** Each piece is a prefab whose root holds a `BoardPiece` at the piece's pivot, with no turn and a scale of 1, and the supplied model under it as `Model`, turned and offset inside the prefab so every piece follows one convention: top up; a frame piece long along X, facing out along +Z, its pivot at the middle of its footprint on the ground; a frame corner piece's rounded corner toward +X +Z; a block quarter piece's pivot at its own center, an inner corner's at its concave corner, both on the ground under the piece's bottom, so the block lift in `BoardArtSettings` is only how far a block is raised or sunk (0 sets it on the ground). Which way each model file faces is settled in its prefab, not in code. Ground, ground base (`GroundBasePiece`, a variant of the ground piece with its model lowered and the base material), wall, door (whose root also holds a `DoorView`, its model and its cube burst, see Leaving), the four frame corners (`Corner1x1Piece`, `Corner1x1RoundPiece`, `CornerLPiece`, `Corner2x2Piece`) and the door arrow (`DoorArrowPiece`, its bottom on its pivot) are in `Prefabs/Board/`, the four block quarter pieces and `Block.prefab` (a `BlockView`) in `Prefabs/Blocks/`. Blocks use the palette's block materials on the `Game/Block` shader (`Shaders/Block.shader`): toon shading on the main light (a ramp from the material's shadow color to its color, a gradient darker toward the block's bottom in the board's space, a rim on faces leaning toward the light's side), drawn only over the board; ground, base, frame and doors are matte URP Lit (see Decisions); doors and their cube burst stand outside the board and use the palette's door materials (URP Lit), set so the light brings them to their blocks' colors as seen; `BoardArtSettings` references the ones the builder uses (the frame corner is `Corner1x1RoundPiece`, the arrow `DoorArrowPiece` with its own light material; the other corners are not used yet). The builder sets each piece's position, its turn about the vertical and its scale, and the palette material on doors and block pieces through `BoardPiece.SetMaterial`. The level editor's preview marks every object of a piece, the model included, as never saved, with the same hide flags as its root (`PreviewBoardPieces.PreviewFlags`). `PooledBoardPieces` keeps one Core `PrefabPool` ([Pooling](../../Base/Systems/Pooling.md)) per piece prefab, made empty the first time that prefab is asked for and idle under the board root's idle object, so the pools fill while the first level is built, need no sizes for the authored boards (an idle pool keeps at most 256 instances, so only a board beyond 16 x 16 cells would destroy and make ground tiles again on a restart) and live and die with the Game scene. It remembers what it hands out; `ReleaseAll` gives back the pieces first (a block's pieces are its children) and then the blocks, and the next level or restart takes the same instances again, making new ones only for what a larger board needs more. A block's tweens are linked to its object with `KillOnDisable`, so a block given back mid-snap or mid-slide stops moving while idle; taking it again sets its pose, which stops any motion left in the same frame.
- **Framing.** `CameraFraming` keeps the camera's rotation and field of view and moves it so that the box of the board (frame included, from the ground to its highest part) fills the window of the screen left by the HUD and the booster slots on one axis and sits in its middle as seen. The window is the screen's safe area less the margins of `CameraFramingSettings`: a side margin as a fraction of the screen's width, and a top and a bottom margin in the UI's reference units, turned into a share of the screen's height as the UI's canvas scaler does (Expand: by the width on phones, by the height on tablets and in a landscape view), so the window stays between the HUD and the slots on every screen; `LevelStage` reads the safe area from `Screen`, as Core's UI does. The camera starts where the board's bounding sphere fits the screen, then five rounds project the box's corners with `WorldToViewportPoint`, step the camera back or forward by the ratio of the board's size on screen to the window's and slide it by the gap between their centers; perspective makes each round overshoot a little, and five leave the board within a pixel of the window. It runs once per level, at the screen's aspect at that moment, so every level is seen from the same angle, as large as it fits.
- **Input.** The board takes touches through the EventSystem (the only one is in the Boot scene, with the Input System UI module, so mouse and touch work the same). `BoardInputView` sits with one `BoxCollider` on the board root's object, so the collider is in the board's own space by construction; `BoardInteraction.Begin` fits it to each level's `BoardLayout.Box`; the camera's `PhysicsRaycaster` finds it. The view handles pointer down, drag and up for the first pointer and the primary button only, turns the drag threshold off and forwards screen positions; the EventSystem sends a drag and a release to the object that was pressed, so a finger that leaves the board keeps dragging and a release outside it still arrives. A new `Listen` (every level) forgets a pointer held from the level before. UI drawn over the board (Core's screens are Screen Space Overlay canvases, whose raycast results the EventSystem sorts before physics ones) takes a press first, so the board never sees it. `TouchPlane` turns a screen position into the point on the plane of the blocks' tops in the board root's local space (the ray is moved into that space first), so `BoardLayout.LocalToBoard` stays right for a moved, turned or scaled board root. The raycaster has `maxRayIntersections` 4, so it raycasts into a fixed array instead of allocating with `RaycastAll` every frame.
- **Session.** `PlaySession.Start(level, onWon, onFailed)` gives the pieces of the board before back to the pools, builds a new `LevelAttempt` ([Level attempt](LevelAttempt.md)), shows the level through `LevelStage` under the board root with the pooled pieces and the scene's camera, and hands the layout and block views to `BoardInteraction`. Each frame it ticks the attempt with the frame time and reads the outcome it returns. The first frame that outcome is no longer `Playing`, the attempt is not paused and the board is settled (`BoardInteraction.IsSettled`: no block view is still sliding out, `BlockView.IsLeaving`) is the one place a level ends, for a win (set by the touch that takes the last block out) and a fail (set by the tick that reaches zero) alike. So the win popup opens after the last block's slide out has ended, and never over the settings popup. Then the session stops running, the interaction puts a held block back, and the session calls `onWon` or `onFailed` once. `Start` and the level's end cancel a booster in use ([Boosters](Boosters.md)). A `Start` while a level plays abandons that attempt without calling back (restart and retry go through it, so no fail popup opens for the attempt left). It stops running before calling back, so the callback can start the next level; it keeps the ended attempt, so the HUD still shows the time the level ended with.
- **Pause.** `Pause()` pauses the attempt (timer and board together) and puts a held block back; `Resume()` resumes the attempt. A block already sliding out finishes its slide while paused, but the level does not end until the pause is over.
- **HUD.** `LevelHud` is a Core screen, opened once and kept over every level; `Show` sets the level number, the coin count and the two button callbacks, which it keeps (it never closes itself). It pulls the time: each frame it reads `PlaySession.RemainingSeconds`, rounds it up to whole seconds (`CountdownText`), and only when the shown second changes writes mm:ss into a fixed char buffer given to TMP with `SetCharArray`, so the countdown allocates nothing. The time shows the limit before the first touch, counts down after it and stops while paused. When the shown second changes, the timer's `TimeWarning` flashes a red overlay over the time box once if 30 seconds or fewer are left (`TimeWarningSettings`), so the flashing follows the countdown and stops with it. The coins are a `CoinCounter`, which shows a number of its own: it lags behind the wallet while coins fly to it and gets the real balance with every `Show`.
- **Popups.** `LevelCompletePopup.Show(coins, onContinue)` and `LevelFailedPopup.Show(onRetry, onHome)` (retry is the green button under the "Out of time!" box, home the close button in the panel's corner, as in the reference) are on Core's popup layer and open with the shared entrance ([Popups](Popups.md)). On a button they clear their callbacks and call the chosen one, so a double tap does nothing; unlike Core's default they do not close themselves, since every choice covers the screen first: `GameEntry` closes them under the fade, or the scene switch to Home removes them, and their dimmer never drops over the board.
- **Win entrance.** The complete popup has no panel, as in the reference: over the dim the title, a large coin (the supplied art has no coin pile or rays) and the continue button, which shows the reward with a coin, pop in one after the other, and confetti in the block colors bursts from the lower corners. The confetti is two ParticleSystems drawn in the UI by UI Particle (`UIParticle` on their parent, [Decisions](../Decisions.md)), with play on awake, so they burst each time the popup opens and no code starts them. `UIParticle` scales with the canvas (auto scaling `UIParticle`, so it never changes its own transform and the prefab keeps its values) and emits from each emitter's own place (position mode `Absolute`); a particle unit is ten reference pixels.
- **Coin flow.** On continue `GameEntry` saves the reward and the run's progress, then asks the Coins feature's `CoinFlight` to show the coins flying from the popup's `RewardOrigin` (its continue button) to the HUD's `CoinCounter` ([Coins](Coins.md)); the popup itself knows nothing about coins. The next level is played covered when the last coin lands.
- **What blocks the board.** The HUD's root and labels are not raycast targets, so a touch between its buttons reaches the board; its buttons take theirs. Every popup has a full-screen dimmer (black, 70 % alpha) as its first child, a raycast target over the whole screen, so while a popup is open neither the board nor the HUD takes a touch. Core's overlay canvases sort before the camera's physics raycaster in the EventSystem.
- **Layout.** The screens are variants of Core's `UIScreenTemplate`; their elements hang under `Content`, which Core fits to the safe area at run time ([UI](../../Base/Systems/UI.md)). Screens are built from shared prefabs: the buttons of [Buttons](Buttons.md), the popup panel and dimmer of [Popups](Popups.md), the coin counter of [Coins](Coins.md), and `BoosterSlot` and `Confetti` in `Play/Prefabs/UI/`; a change to one of them reaches every screen. Every screen follows the reference's arrangement ([Reference](../Reference.md), UI layout) with the supplied art. The HUD anchors to the top of `Content`: on the left one bar with the level number in its circle and the time under a "Time" caption, on the right restart above pause and the coins under them with a "+" (a button with press feedback and no function, as the case allows), and at the bottom of `Content` the booster slots ([Boosters](Boosters.md)). The bar, the buttons, the coins and the slots are sized and placed after the reference's measured HUD ([Reference](../Reference.md), UI layout), scaled down on the HUD rather than in their shared prefabs. Art comes from `Assets/Art/UI/`, with 9-slice borders on the stretched sprites; every text is set in Lilita One (`Assets/Plugins/LilitaOne/`, a static TextMesh Pro font asset with the printable ASCII characters) through three material presets after the reference's lettering: `Navy` (a navy edge and drop shadow, for white text on blue), `Green` (the same in dark green, for green buttons) and `Title` (a dark inner edge and a thick orange outer edge, for the level complete title). The edge is the preset's outline, the shadow and the outer edge its underlay, so no text is doubled for a shadow. The UI is laid out at 1080 x 1920 reference units: `GameUIRoot` fits that reference into the screen (the canvas scaler's Expand mode), so a taller phone is scaled by its width and only adds height, and a wider tablet is scaled by its height and only adds width, where the HUD's left and right groups move apart and the board grows. UI prefabs open in prefab mode inside `Editor/PrefabMode/Scenes/UIPrefabEnvironment.unity` (the project's UI Environment), which holds `GameUIRoot`, so prefab mode shows the game's scale; set the Game view to 1080 x 1920 to see the reference layout. Prefab mode shows `Content` unfitted; a notched phone is checked in play mode in the Device Simulator, with the device chosen before entering play mode.
- **Restart and next level.** Every `Start` builds the level again from its data: a new attempt, and every piece and block view of the board before back in its pool before the new board takes from them, so nothing from the previous attempt stays and a restart instantiates nothing.
- **Drag.** `BoardInteraction` talks only to the attempt. On press it passes the finger position in cells, projected by `TouchPlane` (`Grab`), which picks the block and starts the timer, or makes a block at its door leave at once; on each move it gives the returned pose to the block view as its target; on release the attempt returns the held block, which snaps onto its cell with a short ease, as the reference does. When a pose, from a move or a press, says the block left, the block leaves through its door (see Leaving).
- **Held block.** A grab switches on the block's outline, a fork of Quick Outline ([Decisions](../Decisions.md)) on the block's root, off on release and on leaving. After a level is built, `BoardInteraction.Begin` gives every block view the shared mask and fill materials (`BlockOutlineMaterials`, made from `BlockOutlineSettings` and owned by the Game scope); the view hands the outline the renderers of its pieces, which `BlockPieceBuilder` handed to it while building, and the outline keeps two material arrays per renderer, with and without the mask and fill, so a grab only swaps arrays and allocates nothing. The mask pass marks every piece in the stencil and the fill pass draws the pieces pushed out along the smoothed normals in UV3 where nothing is marked, so only the rim around the whole block shows, at an even width on screen, drawn over everything. `BlockPlatesImport` (Game.Editor) writes the smoothed normals at import. The outline outlines a piece's whole mesh, so the block pieces are the flat plates of `BlockPlates.fbx` ([Decisions](../Decisions.md)), with nothing hidden below the ground. The width is in Quick Outline's units, which keep it even on screen. The level editor's preview never holds a block and gives no materials.
- **Door rest pose.** `DoorView` reads its model's and burst's rest pose on its first `Init`, not in `Awake`: the level editor's preview builds doors in edit mode, where `Awake` does not run, and a pooled door may be mid-drop when it is taken again.
- **Leaving.** The block view snaps onto the cell in front of the door from where it is drawn, which may trail that cell after a fast drag, with the release's snap (`BlockMotionSettings.SnapDuration` and ease), as the reference does, and then slides out from rest at a constant acceleration (a quadratic ease in), over the door as it drops, by its own length across the door's side (`BoardBlock.ExtentAcross`, read from the attempt's board, since a block made at run time has no entry in the level's data) plus the frame's width; `BlockMotionSettings.SlideDuration` gives the time, so a longer block takes longer. A block is outside the board only while it leaves, and its shader draws it only within the board and its frame, so it is cut away at the frame's outer face as it passes, with no state of its own; the back faces seen through the cut are drawn in a flat shade of its color. As the snap starts, the door's `DoorView.LetThrough(reach, gone)`, timed from the snap's start, drops the door's model and the arrow at once by `BoardArtSettings.DoorDrop` (down to a flat strip of its color), plays the burst from the moment the block's front reaches the cut until it is gone, and raises both again a moment after. The burst is a particle system under the door's root, on the door's outer face at the blocks' half height, that scales with the door's length (shape scaling) and throws cubes in the door's material (the built-in cube mesh) out of the board with spin, shrinking away: they leave fast and straight out from a rectangle across the door, a velocity limit brakes them to a drift that noise scatters, and with no gravity they spread away from the door rather than drop; only the model drops, so the burst stays on the line. The door's tweens are linked to its object like the block's.
- **Catching up.** `BoardInteraction.CatchUp()` brings every block view to the logic's state: it walks the attempt's board (`LevelAttempt.Board`, read-only) and hands each view its `BoardBlock`; the view compares it with what it shows and plays the difference. Whoever changes the board calls it, with no event in between ([Decisions](../Decisions.md)): today the drag, after a block leaves through its door. For now a view only hides a block that is gone without leaving through a door (a break); ice left, a changed shape, a burst on a break and views for blocks made at run time come with phase 7's ice and boosters. A block sliding out through its door is gone in the logic already and is left to its slide.
- **Touch modes.** `BoardInteraction` routes the board's touches by mode ([Decisions](../Decisions.md)): `EnterDrag` (the drag above, the mode `Begin` starts in), `EnterPick(onPicked)` (a press gives its board position to the aiming booster, nothing is held) and `EnterBusy` (no touches while a booster's shot plays, and `IsSettled` is false meanwhile, so a level won by a shot ends after it). Switching re-listens on the input view, which forgets a pointer held in the mode before. For the boosters' overlay and shots it also gives the board's rectangle on screen (`ScreenRect`, the box with its frame projected through `TouchPlane.ToScreen`), a cell's screen point at a height (`ScreenPointOf`) and `VanishGone`, which makes the views of blocks gone without a door move to a point and shrink away ([Boosters](Boosters.md)).
- **Motion.** `BlockView` approaches its target with `ExponentialFollow`: each frame it covers `1 - e^(-ln2 · dt / halfLife)` of the remaining distance, so the distance halves every half-life whatever the frame rate, and a long frame never passes the target. Only the drag uses it. The view puts itself on the target when closer than 0.001 units and stops updating; a new target starts it again. The one-shot motions are DOTween tweens linked to the block's object; starting any motion stops the one before, so grabbing a block mid-snap takes it over at once.

## Rules & invariants
- Decisions are in `Game.Logic` (`LevelAttempt` over `Board` and `BlockDrag`); the views only show poses.
- The board is built once per attempt from pooled pieces; nothing in the drag path or the per-frame tick allocates.
- The session owns the board: it alone gives the pieces back. The builder, the framing and the stage hold no board.
- A level ends once: after the callback the session ticks no attempt until the next `Start`.
- A level ends only on a settled board and never while paused, so no popup opens over a block still leaving or over the settings popup; between the decision and the end, the HUD's restart and pause do nothing.
- The HUD and popups only show and forward buttons; what follows a button is `GameEntry`'s.
- The HUD's countdown and the popups' buttons allocate nothing per frame.
- Colors come from `ColorPalette` (a block and a door material per color id, in the same color), sizes and piece prefabs from `BoardArtSettings`, the framing margins from `CameraFramingSettings`, the drag half-life, the snap time and ease, the leave acceleration and the door's drop and rise from `BlockMotionSettings`, given to each block view and door view through `Init`, the coins of a win from `WinRewardSettings`, the time warning from `TimeWarningSettings`.
- A reward and the level progress are written and saved when the player presses continue, before the coins fly; the flow only shows them, so a game killed mid-flow keeps both.

## Known gaps
- The dropped door lies in the frame's shadow, so its strip reads darker than its color until the light's shadows are tuned.
- No sound on buttons.
- During a diagonal drag a block can be drawn up to half a cell into the cell diagonal to it, when both straight neighbours are free but the diagonal one is not.
