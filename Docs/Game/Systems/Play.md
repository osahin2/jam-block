# Play

> Summary: Shows a level on the board and lets the player drag blocks out through their doors. The play session shows the level (board pieces from pools, the camera framed to the board's size) and runs the level's time; the board interaction takes the board's touches from the EventSystem, turns them into board positions, asks the level attempt for whole-cell steps and a sub-cell offset, and moves the block views. When the level is won or failed, and the last block has finished sliding out, the session tells its caller once. Over the board the HUD shows the level number, the time left and the coins, with restart and pause; the Game scene opens the level complete popup (the coin reward, then the next level) or the level failed popup (retry or home), and pause opens the settings popup over the stopped level.

## Code
- Folder: `Assets/Game/Runtime/Play/` (`Scripts/Flow/`, `Scripts/Presentation/` with `Board/`, `Blocks/`, `Camera/`, `Input/`, `Scripts/UI/`, `Scripts/Settings/`; assets in `Installers/`, `Settings/`, `Prefabs/` with `Board/`, `Blocks/`, `UI/`, `Materials/` with `Board/`, `Blocks/`), `Assets/Game/Runtime/Bootstrap/` (`GameScope`, `GameEntry`, `Scenes/Game.unity`)
- Assembly: `Game`
- Entry points: `PlaySession.Start(level, onWon, onFailed)`, `Pause()`, `Resume()`, `RemainingSeconds`, `IsDecided` (`Play/Scripts/Flow/PlaySession.cs`); the screens `LevelHud`, `LevelCompletePopup`, `LevelFailedPopup` (`Play/Scripts/UI/`), listed in `Play/Settings/PlayScreens.asset`; `PlayInstaller` (`Play/Installers/PlayInstaller.asset`). `BoardInteraction` (`Play/Scripts/Flow/BoardInteraction.cs`) is used only by the session. `LevelStage.Show` (`Play/Scripts/Presentation/Board/LevelStage.cs`) is also called by the level editor's scene preview ([Level editor](LevelEditor.md)), so the preview shows a level the way the game does.

## How it works
- **Scene.** `Game.unity` holds the camera (with a `PhysicsRaycaster`), a light, Core's `UIRoot`, the `Board` object with `BoardRoot` (the level is built under it; its idle object holds the pooled pieces no level uses) and the board's input (a `BoxCollider` and `BoardInputView` on the same object), and `GameScope`, a child of the Boot scope ([App flow](AppFlow.md)), which registers Core UI on the scene's `UIRoot`, the camera, the board root and the board input and runs its installer list (`PlayInstaller`, `PreferencesInstaller`). `GameEntry` is the scene's flow, one method per step, the screens' buttons leading from one to the next:
  - **Start** chooses the run once ([App flow](AppFlow.md)): the level the level editor asked to play (`EditorPlayRequest`, taken once; never in a build) or the player's own run. It opens the HUD and plays the run's level.
  - **Play** starts the session with the run's level and shows the HUD with the level number, the coin balance, restart (Play again) and pause. Restart and pause do nothing once the session `IsDecided` (won or failed, the last block perhaps still sliding out), so neither can throw away a win before its popup opens.
  - **Pause** pauses the session and opens the settings popup ([Preferences](Preferences.md)); its close resumes the level, its home goes to the Home scene.
  - **Won** opens the level complete popup with the reward of `WinRewardSettings`; its continue adds the coins to the wallet, advances the run and plays its next level.
  - **Failed** opens the level failed popup; retry plays the same level again, home goes to the Home scene.
  All of it happens in this scene, without a scene switch, until home is chosen.
- **Layout.** `BoardLayout` puts the board on the XZ plane centered on the origin, row 0 at the far edge (+Z). It works in the board root's local space, the space the board is built in: it converts cells and drag poses to local positions and local points to board positions in cells. It is made from the art settings, so it also holds the board's box (`Box`: the board with its frame, from the ground to the blocks' tops), the one box the camera frames and the input covers.
- **Showing a level.** `LevelStage.Show(level, parent, pieces, camera)` is the one way a level is shown: it makes the layout, builds the board under the parent from the piece source and frames the camera. It makes its `BoardBuilder` (with its `BlockPieceBuilder`) and `CameraFraming` from the settings assets it is given, so the level editor names only `LevelStage`, the settings and a piece source, and a new dependency of the builder or the framing changes one constructor. The caller owns the parent and the pieces: the session builds under the scene's board root from the pools, the scene preview under its own unsaved object from plain copies.
- **Building.** `BoardBuilder.Build(level, layout, parent, pieces)` places a ground tile per cell, the frame (two half-cell wall pieces per edge cell, a door piece per cell a door covers, a wall piece on each corner) and one `BlockView` per block under the parent, in its local space. It takes every piece from the `IBoardPieceSource` it is given (`Take(prefab, parent)`, an instance active under the parent with the prefab's local pose) and creates, destroys and remembers nothing itself. `BlockPieceBuilder` builds a block from the supplied quarter pieces ([Supplied assets](../SuppliedAssets.md)): the piece of each quarter follows from its side and diagonal neighbours, and one inner-corner piece covers the three quarters around a concave corner; a quarter piece is placed at its quarter's center and an inner corner at its concave corner, turned about that point. Frame pieces are turned to face out of their side; the corners are wall pieces.
- **Pieces.** Each piece is a prefab whose root holds a `BoardPiece` at the piece's pivot, with no turn and a scale of 1, and the supplied model under it as `Model`, turned and offset inside the prefab so every piece follows one convention: top up; a frame piece long along X, facing out along +Z, its pivot at the middle of its footprint on the ground; a block quarter piece's pivot at its own center, an inner corner's at its concave corner, both on the ground under the piece's bottom, so the block lift in `BoardArtSettings` is only how far a block is raised or sunk (0 sets it on the ground). Which way each model file faces is settled in its prefab, not in code. Ground, wall, door, the four frame corners (`Corner1x1Piece`, `Corner1x1RoundPiece`, `CornerLPiece`, `Corner2x2Piece`) and the door arrow (`DoorArrowPiece`, its bottom on its pivot) are in `Prefabs/Board/`, the four block quarter pieces and `Block.prefab` (a `BlockView`) in `Prefabs/Blocks/`; `BoardArtSettings` references the ones the builder uses (the corners and the arrow are not used yet). The builder sets each piece's position, its turn about the vertical and its scale, and the palette material on doors and block pieces through `BoardPiece.SetMaterial`. The level editor's preview marks every object of a piece, the model included, as never saved, with the same hide flags as its root (`PreviewBoardPieces.PreviewFlags`). `PooledBoardPieces` keeps one Core `PrefabPool` ([Pooling](../../Base/Systems/Pooling.md)) per piece prefab, made empty the first time that prefab is asked for and idle under the board root's idle object, so the pools fill while the first level is built, need no sizes for the authored boards (an idle pool keeps at most 256 instances, so only a board beyond 16 x 16 cells would destroy and make ground tiles again on a restart) and live and die with the Game scene. It remembers what it hands out; `ReleaseAll` gives back the pieces first (a block's pieces are its children) and then the blocks, and the next level or restart takes the same instances again, making new ones only for what a larger board needs more. A block's tweens are linked to its object with `KillOnDisable`, so a block given back mid-snap or mid-slide stops moving while idle; taking it again sets its pose, which stops any motion left in the same frame.
- **Framing.** `CameraFraming` keeps the camera's rotation and field of view and moves it along its view axis to or from the board's center, just far enough that the box of the board (frame included, from the ground to the block tops) fits the screen inside the margins of `CameraFramingSettings` (a side margin and a top-and-bottom margin, as fractions of the screen). Each corner gives the distance it needs across and up the screen at the camera's aspect; the largest is used, so every level is seen from the same angle, as large as it fits. It runs once per level, at the screen's aspect at that moment.
- **Input.** The board takes touches through the EventSystem (the only one is in the Boot scene, with the Input System UI module, so mouse and touch work the same). `BoardInputView` sits with one `BoxCollider` on the board root's object, so the collider is in the board's own space by construction; `BoardInteraction.Begin` fits it to each level's `BoardLayout.Box`; the camera's `PhysicsRaycaster` finds it. The view handles pointer down, drag and up for the first pointer and the primary button only, turns the drag threshold off and forwards screen positions; the EventSystem sends a drag and a release to the object that was pressed, so a finger that leaves the board keeps dragging and a release outside it still arrives. A new `Listen` (every level) forgets a pointer held from the level before. UI drawn over the board (Core's screens are Screen Space Overlay canvases, whose raycast results the EventSystem sorts before physics ones) takes a press first, so the board never sees it. `TouchPlane` turns a screen position into the point on the plane of the blocks' tops in the board root's local space (the ray is moved into that space first), so `BoardLayout.LocalToBoard` stays right for a moved, turned or scaled board root. The raycaster has `maxRayIntersections` 4, so it raycasts into a fixed array instead of allocating with `RaycastAll` every frame.
- **Session.** `PlaySession.Start(level, onWon, onFailed)` gives the pieces of the board before back to the pools, builds a new `LevelAttempt` ([Level attempt](LevelAttempt.md)), shows the level through `LevelStage` under the board root with the pooled pieces and the scene's camera, and hands the layout and block views to `BoardInteraction`. Each frame it ticks the attempt with the frame time and reads the outcome it returns. The first frame that outcome is no longer `Playing`, the attempt is not paused and the board is settled (`BoardInteraction.IsSettled`: no block view is still sliding out, `BlockView.IsLeaving`) is the one place a level ends, for a win (set by the touch that takes the last block out) and a fail (set by the tick that reaches zero) alike. So the win popup opens after the last block's slide out has ended, and never over the settings popup. Then the session stops running, the interaction puts a held block back, and the session calls `onWon` or `onFailed` once. It stops running before calling back, so the callback can start the next level; it keeps the ended attempt, so the HUD still shows the time the level ended with.
- **Pause.** `Pause()` pauses the attempt (timer and board together) and puts a held block back; `Resume()` resumes the attempt. A block already sliding out finishes its slide while paused, but the level does not end until the pause is over.
- **HUD.** `LevelHud` is a Core screen, opened once and kept over every level; `Show` sets the level number, the coin count and the two button callbacks, which it keeps (it never closes itself). It pulls the time: each frame it reads `PlaySession.RemainingSeconds`, rounds it up to whole seconds (`CountdownText`), and only when the shown second changes writes mm:ss into a fixed char buffer given to TMP with `SetCharArray`, so the countdown allocates nothing. The time shows the limit before the first touch, counts down after it and stops while paused.
- **Popups.** `LevelCompletePopup.Show(coins, onContinue)` and `LevelFailedPopup.Show(onRetry, onHome)` are on Core's popup layer. On a button they clear their callbacks, close, then call the chosen one, so a double tap does nothing ([UI](../../Base/Systems/UI.md)).
- **What blocks the board.** The HUD's root and labels are not raycast targets, so a touch between its buttons reaches the board; its buttons take theirs. Every popup has a full-screen dimmer (black, 70 % alpha) as its first child, a raycast target over the whole screen, so while a popup is open neither the board nor the HUD takes a touch. Core's overlay canvases sort before the camera's physics raycaster in the EventSystem.
- **Layout.** The screens are variants of Core's `UIScreenTemplate`; their elements hang under `Content`, which Core fits to the safe area at run time ([UI](../../Base/Systems/UI.md)). The HUD anchors to the top of `Content`: pause and restart on the left, the level number and the timer in the middle, the coins on the right. Art comes from `Assets/Art/UI/`, with 9-slice borders on the stretched sprites. Prefab mode shows `Content` unfitted; a notched phone is checked in play mode in the Device Simulator, with the device chosen before entering play mode.
- **Restart and next level.** Every `Start` builds the level again from its data: a new attempt, and every piece and block view of the board before back in its pool before the new board takes from them, so nothing from the previous attempt stays and a restart instantiates nothing.
- **Drag.** `BoardInteraction` talks only to the attempt. On press it passes the finger position in cells, projected by `TouchPlane` (`Grab`), which picks the block and starts the timer, or makes a block at its door leave at once; on each move it gives the returned pose to the block view as its target; on release the attempt returns the held block, which snaps onto its cell with a short ease, as the reference does. When a pose, from a move or a press, says the block left, one DOTween sequence snaps it onto the cell in front of the door, holds there for a moment, slides it out through the door by the leave distance of the motion settings and hides it.
- **Motion.** `BlockView` approaches its target with `ExponentialFollow`: each frame it covers `1 - e^(-ln2 · dt / halfLife)` of the remaining distance, so the distance halves every half-life whatever the frame rate, and a long frame never passes the target. Only the drag uses it. The view puts itself on the target when closer than 0.001 units and stops updating; a new target starts it again. The one-shot motions are DOTween tweens linked to the block's object; starting any motion stops the one before, so grabbing a block mid-snap takes it over at once.

## Rules & invariants
- Decisions are in `Game.Logic` (`LevelAttempt` over `Board` and `BlockDrag`); the views only show poses.
- The board is built once per attempt from pooled pieces; nothing in the drag path or the per-frame tick allocates.
- The session owns the board: it alone gives the pieces back. The builder, the framing and the stage hold no board.
- A level ends once: after the callback the session ticks no attempt until the next `Start`.
- A level ends only on a settled board and never while paused, so no popup opens over a block still leaving or over the settings popup; between the decision and the end, the HUD's restart and pause do nothing.
- The HUD and popups only show and forward buttons; what follows a button is `GameEntry`'s.
- The HUD's countdown and the popups' buttons allocate nothing per frame.
- Colors come from `ColorPalette` (one material per color id), sizes and piece prefabs from `BoardArtSettings`, the framing margins from `CameraFramingSettings`, the drag half-life, the snap time and ease, the hold time and the slide out from `BlockMotionSettings`, given to each block view through `Init`, the coins of a win from `WinRewardSettings`.

## Known gaps
- No outline on the held block, no door animation; a leaving block slides out whole instead of being broken apart by the door.
- The camera frames the board centered on the screen with the same margin above and below; the authored boards are bound by the screen's width and stay clear of the HUD, so no top offset was added ([Decisions](../Decisions.md)). A tall board would need one.
- No show or hide animation on the popups, no coin fly to the HUD, no sound on buttons.
- During a diagonal drag a block can be drawn up to half a cell into the cell diagonal to it, when both straight neighbours are free but the diagonal one is not.
- A door longer than one cell is a row of one-cell door pieces with a visible seam.

## Tests
- `Tests/Play/Drag/BlockDragTests`: offsets toward free and blocked cells, steps past half a cell, several cells at once, stopping at a block, going around an obstacle, the grab point, leaving through a door.
- `Tests/Play/Presentation/Blocks/ExponentialFollowTests`: half the distance after one half-life, two short frames equal one long frame, a long frame does not pass the target.
- `Tests/Play/Presentation/Camera/CameraFramingTests`: the board with its frame fits inside the margins and reaches them, at 1080 x 1920 and 20:9, for the smallest and largest authored boards and a wide one; the camera keeps its rotation and centers the board wherever the board is; a smaller board brings the camera closer.
- `Tests/Play/Presentation/Input/TouchPlaneTests`: the top of a cell, seen on screen and projected back, gives the cell's center on the board, also with the board root moved, turned and scaled; a point beside the board still projects; a ray away from the plane misses.
- `Tests/Play/Presentation/Input/BoardInputViewTests`: press, drag and release go on as screen positions; a second pointer while the first is down, another button, and a drag or release without a press are ignored; a new listener starts with no pointer down; the drag threshold is turned off; `Cover` fits the collider to the box.
- `Tests/Play/Presentation/Board/BoardLayoutTests`: the box covers the cells and the frame, from the ground to the blocks' tops.
- `Tests/Play/Presentation/Board/LevelStageTests` (the project's settings, prefabs and levels): every piece of every catalog level, as built, lies inside the framing margins at 1080 x 1920 and 20:9, so a frame or door raised above the box the framing assumes fails here.
- `Tests/Play/Presentation/Board/PooledBoardPiecesTests` (the project's settings and prefabs): the pools make nothing until asked; `ReleaseAll` gives every piece back inactive; the same level shown again reuses every piece; a smaller level after a larger one keeps nothing of the first and is built from its pieces.
- `Tests/Play/Presentation/Blocks/BlockViewTests`: a view that stopped starts again on a new target; `SetPose` while following puts the block there and stops.
- `Tests/Play/UI/CountdownTextTests`: seconds rounded up and never below zero; mm:ss for five minutes, a minute and a second, single seconds, zero and the largest shown time.
- `Tests/Bootstrap/InstallerAssetTests`: every installer and the settings it references have no unassigned reference (the reward and the screen catalog included).
- The session's end and the Game scene's flow are checked in the editor: press Play, win a level (the complete popup opens once the last block is gone; continue adds the coins and builds the next level, framed to its size), let the time run out (the failed popup; retry starts the same level, home leaves), pause (the time stops, the board takes no touch, close resumes), restart from the HUD; the hierarchy shows the board's pieces under `Board` and the idle ones under `Idle Board Pieces`, with no `(Clone)` count growing.
- Views and input are checked in the editor: open `Game.unity` and press Play; drag blocks, tap a block standing at its door, release a drag outside the board, press a HUD button over the board (the board does not react) and tap the board under an open popup (nothing moves). A block given back mid-motion cannot be tested in EditMode, where DOTween does not update.
