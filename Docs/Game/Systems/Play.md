# Play

> Summary: Shows a level on the board and lets the player drag blocks out through their doors. The play session shows the level (board pieces from pools, the camera framed to the board's size) and runs the level's time; the board interaction turns the pointer into board positions, asks the level attempt for whole-cell steps and a sub-cell offset, and moves the block views. When the level is won or failed the session tells its caller once; the Game scene continues with the next level or plays the same one again. No HUD, win screen or fail popup yet.

## Code
- Folder: `Assets/Game/Runtime/Play/` (`Scripts/Flow/`, `Scripts/Presentation/` with `Board/`, `Blocks/`, `Camera/`, `Input/`, `Scripts/Settings/`; assets in `Installers/`, `Settings/`, `Prefabs/`, `Materials/`), `Assets/Game/Runtime/Bootstrap/` (`GameScope`, `GameEntry`, `Scenes/Game.unity`)
- Assembly: `Game`
- Entry points: `PlaySession.Start(level, onWon, onFailed)` (`Play/Scripts/Flow/PlaySession.cs`); `PlayInstaller` (`Play/Installers/PlayInstaller.asset`). `BoardInteraction` (`Play/Scripts/Flow/BoardInteraction.cs`) is used only by the session. `LevelStage.Show` (`Play/Scripts/Presentation/Board/LevelStage.cs`) is also called by the level editor's scene preview ([Level editor](LevelEditor.md)), so the preview shows a level the way the game does.

## How it works
- **Scene.** `Game.unity` holds the camera, a light, the `Board` object with `BoardRoot` (the level is built under it; its idle object holds the pooled pieces no level uses) and `GameScope`, a child of the Boot scope ([App flow](AppFlow.md)), which registers the camera and the board root and runs its installer list (`PlayInstaller`). `GameEntry` is the scene's flow: it starts the level the level editor asked to play (`EditorPlayRequest`, taken once; never in a build), else the player's current level (`LevelSequence.Current`), and decides what follows a level: a win plays the next level ([App flow](AppFlow.md): the player's run completes their level, a run from the level editor never moves progress), a fail plays the same level again. Both happen in this scene, without a scene switch.
- **Layout.** `BoardLayout` puts the board on the XZ plane centered on the origin, row 0 at the far edge (+Z). It converts cells and drag poses to world positions and world points to board positions in cells.
- **Showing a level.** `LevelStage.Show(level, parent, pieces, camera)` is the one way a level is shown: it makes the layout, builds the board under the parent from the piece source and frames the camera. It makes its `BoardBuilder` (with its `BlockPieceBuilder`) and `CameraFraming` from the settings assets it is given, so the level editor names only `LevelStage`, the settings and a piece source, and a new dependency of the builder or the framing changes one constructor. The caller owns the parent and the pieces: the session builds under the scene's board root from the pools, the scene preview under its own unsaved object from plain copies.
- **Building.** `BoardBuilder.Build(level, layout, parent, pieces)` places a ground tile per cell, the frame (two half-cell wall pieces per edge cell, a door piece per cell a door covers, a wall piece on each corner) and one `BlockView` per block under the parent, in its local space. It takes every piece from the `IBoardPieceSource` it is given (`Take(prefab, parent)`, an instance active under the parent with the prefab's local pose) and creates, destroys and remembers nothing itself. `BlockPieceBuilder` builds a block from the supplied quarter pieces ([Supplied assets](../SuppliedAssets.md)): the piece of each quarter follows from its side and diagonal neighbours, and one inner-corner piece covers the three quarters around a concave corner. Model files are turned per file (blocks lie flat with a 90 degree turn around X, frame pieces turn their cap up).
- **Pieces.** Each piece is a prefab in `Prefabs/` (one mesh, scale 1: four block quarter pieces, ground, wall, door) and `Block.prefab` (a `BlockView`), referenced by `BoardArtSettings`; the builder sets each piece's pose and scale, and the palette material on doors and block pieces. `PooledBoardPieces` keeps one Core `PrefabPool` ([Pooling](../../Base/Systems/Pooling.md)) per piece prefab, made empty the first time that prefab is asked for and idle under the board root's idle object, so the pools fill while the first level is built, need no sizes for the authored boards (an idle pool keeps at most 256 instances, so only a board beyond 16 x 16 cells would destroy and make ground tiles again on a restart) and live and die with the Game scene. It remembers what it hands out; `ReleaseAll` gives back the pieces first (a block's pieces are its children) and then the blocks, and the next level or restart takes the same instances again, making new ones only for what a larger board needs more. A block's tweens are linked to its object with `KillOnDisable`, so a block given back mid-snap or mid-slide stops moving while idle; taking it again sets its pose, which stops any motion left in the same frame.
- **Framing.** `CameraFraming` keeps the camera's rotation and field of view and moves it along its view axis to or from the board's center, just far enough that the box of the board (frame included, from the ground to the block tops) fits the screen inside the margins of `CameraFramingSettings` (a side margin and a top-and-bottom margin, as fractions of the screen). Each corner gives the distance it needs across and up the screen at the camera's aspect; the largest is used, so every level is seen from the same angle, as large as it fits. It runs once per level, at the screen's aspect at that moment.
- **Input.** `BoardPointer` (a VContainer `ITickable`) reads `Pointer.current`, so mouse and touch work the same, and casts a ray onto the plane of the blocks' tops. It calls back on press, move and release.
- **Session.** `PlaySession.Start(level, onWon, onFailed)` gives the pieces of the board before back to the pools, builds a new `LevelAttempt` ([Level attempt](LevelAttempt.md)), shows the level through `LevelStage` under the board root with the pooled pieces and the scene's camera, and hands the layout and block views to `BoardInteraction`. Each frame it ticks the attempt with the frame time and reads the outcome it returns. The first frame that outcome is no longer `Playing` is the one place a level ends, for a win (set by the touch that takes the last block out) and a fail (set by the tick that reaches zero) alike: the session forgets the attempt, the interaction puts a held block back, and the session calls `onWon` or `onFailed` once. It forgets the attempt before calling back, so the callback can start the next level.
- **Restart and next level.** Every `Start` builds the level again from its data: a new attempt, and every piece and block view of the board before back in its pool before the new board takes from them, so nothing from the previous attempt stays and a restart instantiates nothing.
- **Drag.** `BoardInteraction` talks only to the attempt. On press it passes the finger position in cells (`Grab`), which picks the block and starts the timer, or makes a block at its door leave at once; on each move it gives the returned pose to the block view as its target; on release the attempt returns the held block, which snaps onto its cell with a short ease, as the reference does. When a pose, from a move or a press, says the block left, one DOTween sequence snaps it onto the cell in front of the door, holds there for a moment, slides it out through the door by the leave distance of the motion settings and hides it.
- **Motion.** `BlockView` approaches its target with `ExponentialFollow`: each frame it covers `1 - e^(-ln2 · dt / halfLife)` of the remaining distance, so the distance halves every half-life whatever the frame rate, and a long frame never passes the target. Only the drag uses it. The view puts itself on the target when closer than 0.001 units and stops updating; a new target starts it again. The one-shot motions are DOTween tweens linked to the block's object; starting any motion stops the one before, so grabbing a block mid-snap takes it over at once.

## Rules & invariants
- Decisions are in `Game.Logic` (`LevelAttempt` over `Board` and `BlockDrag`); the views only show poses.
- The board is built once per attempt from pooled pieces; nothing in the drag path or the per-frame tick allocates.
- The session owns the board: it alone gives the pieces back. The builder, the framing and the stage hold no board.
- A level ends once: after the callback the session ticks no attempt until the next `Start`.
- Colors come from `ColorPalette` (one material per color id), sizes and piece prefabs from `BoardArtSettings`, the framing margins from `CameraFramingSettings`, the drag half-life, the snap time and ease, the hold time and the slide out from `BlockMotionSettings`, given to each block view through `Init`.

## Known gaps
- No outline on the held block, no door animation; a leaving block slides out whole instead of being broken apart by the door.
- The camera frames the board centered on the screen; the HUD (step 5) may need more room at the top than the bottom, which would add an offset.
- During a diagonal drag a block can be drawn up to half a cell into the cell diagonal to it, when both straight neighbours are free but the diagonal one is not.
- A door longer than one cell is a row of one-cell door pieces with a visible seam.
- `BoardPointer` polls `Pointer.current` and ignores the EventSystem, so a press on UI over the board also reaches the board; it moves to EventSystem handlers on one board-sized collider with the HUD (phase 4).
- The timer runs but is not shown; there is no HUD, win screen, fail popup or pause yet (phase 4 step 5), and no way back to Home from a level.
- A win builds the next level at once, so the last block's slide out is cut short; the win screen will come between them.

## Tests
- `Tests/Play/Drag/BlockDragTests`: offsets toward free and blocked cells, steps past half a cell, several cells at once, stopping at a block, going around an obstacle, the grab point, leaving through a door.
- `Tests/Play/Presentation/Blocks/ExponentialFollowTests`: half the distance after one half-life, two short frames equal one long frame, a long frame does not pass the target.
- `Tests/Play/Presentation/Camera/CameraFramingTests`: the board with its frame fits inside the margins and reaches them, at 1080 x 1920 and 20:9, for the smallest and largest authored boards and a wide one; the camera keeps its rotation and centers the board wherever the board is; a smaller board brings the camera closer.
- `Tests/Play/Presentation/Board/LevelStageTests` (the project's settings, prefabs and levels): every piece of every catalog level, as built, lies inside the framing margins at 1080 x 1920 and 20:9, so a frame or door raised above the box the framing assumes fails here.
- `Tests/Play/Presentation/Board/PooledBoardPiecesTests` (the project's settings and prefabs): the pools make nothing until asked; `ReleaseAll` gives every piece back inactive; the same level shown again reuses every piece; a smaller level after a larger one keeps nothing of the first and is built from its pieces.
- `Tests/Play/Presentation/Blocks/BlockViewTests`: a view that stopped starts again on a new target; `SetPose` while following puts the block there and stops.
- `Tests/Bootstrap/InstallerAssetTests`: every installer and the settings it references have no unassigned reference.
- The session's end and the Game scene's flow are checked in the editor: press Play, win a level (the next one is built, framed to its size), let the time run out (the same level starts again); the hierarchy shows the board's pieces under `Board` and the idle ones under `Idle Board Pieces`, with no `(Clone)` count growing.
- Views and input are checked in the editor: open `Game.unity` and press Play. A block given back mid-motion cannot be tested in EditMode, where DOTween does not update.
